2. Main Real-Process Case Study (Track E)

2.1 Problem Discovery & Practical Inefficiency

In modern electric power grids (e.g., California ISO - CAISO), high penetration of grid-scale solar photovoltaic (PV) generation produces the characteristic "Duck Curve": wholesale electricity prices plummet or go negative during midday hours due to solar surplus, followed by steep evening price spikes ($100+/MWh) when solar drops and system net demand surges.

Utility-scale Battery Energy Storage Systems (BESS) are deployed to perform energy arbitrage (charging when electricity is cheap/clean, discharging when prices are high). However, standard industrial baselines rely on heuristic rule-based threshold controllers (charging when price drops below the 25th percentile, discharging above the 75th percentile).

Documented Inefficiencies of the Baseline: 1. Suboptimal Arbitrage Timing: Greedy thresholding ignores multi-hour lookahead, often filling the battery too early and missing steeper price spikes later in the cycle. 2. Accelerated Cell Degradation: Uncoordinated charging creates excessive throughput cycles, incurring heavy cell degradation costs ($C_{deg} \approx \$3.5/\text{MWh}$). 3. Feeder Violations: Coincident solar injection and battery discharge can violate distribution feeder capacity limits ($P_{feeder} \le 40\,\text{MW}$), triggering curtailment penalties.

2.2 Mathematical Formulation

We formulate the daily dispatch of a grid-tied utility battery storage system as a constrained optimization problem over a discrete 24-hour horizon ($T = 24$, $\Delta t = 1\,\text{hour}$).

Decision Variables:

- $P_{ch}(t) \in [0, P_{\max}]$: Battery charging power at hour $t$ (MW). - $P_{dis}(t) \in [0, P_{\max}]$: Battery discharging power at hour $t$ (MW). - $SoC(t) \in [SoC_{\min}, SoC_{\max}]$: Battery State of Charge at hour $t$ (normalized $[0, 1]$).

Total decision variables: $3 \times 24 = 72$ variables ($x \in \mathbb{R}^{72}$).

Objective Function:

Minimize total daily net operating cost (equivalent to maximizing net arbitrage profit): $$\min_{\{P_{ch}, P_{dis}, SoC\}} \mathcal{J} = \sum_{t=0}^{T-1} \Big[ \text{Price}(t) \cdot \big(P_{ch}(t) - P_{dis}(t)\big)\Delta t + C_{deg} \cdot \big(P_{ch}(t) + P_{dis}(t)\big)\Delta t + C_{carbon} \cdot \text{GridEmissions}(t) \Big]$$

Where: - $\text{Price}(t)$: Hourly wholesale LMP electricity price (\$/MWh). - $C_{deg} = \$3.5/\text{MWh}$: Levelized battery cell degradation wear cost per MWh throughput. - $C_{carbon} = \$0.03/\text{kg CO}_2$: Grid carbon emission intensity penalty.

Constraints:

1. Dynamic State-of-Charge Conservation (Equality Constraint): $$SoC(t+1) = SoC(t) + \frac{\eta_{ch} P_{ch}(t) \Delta t}{E_{cap}} - \frac{P_{dis}(t) \Delta t}{\eta_{dis} E_{cap}}, \quad \forall t \in \{0, \dots, T-1\}$$ Where $E_{cap} = 100\,\text{MWh}$ is nameplate capacity, and $\eta_{ch} = \eta_{dis} = 0.95$ is round-trip single-leg efficiency.

2. Operating State-of-Charge Boundaries: $$SoC_{\min} \le SoC(t) \le SoC_{\max}, \quad \forall t \in \{1, \dots, T\}$$ Set to $SoC_{\min} = 0.10$ (10%) and $SoC_{\max} = 0.90$ (90%) to prevent lithium dendrite plating and thermal instability.

3. Power Inverter Ratings: $$0 \le P_{ch}(t) \le P_{\max}, \quad 0 \le P_{dis}(t) \le P_{\max}, \quad \forall t \in \{0, \dots, T-1\}$$ Where $P_{\max} = 25\,\text{MW}$ (C/4 nominal discharge rate).

4. Distribution Feeder Injection Limits: $$P_{dis}(t) - P_{ch}(t) + P_{solar}(t) \le P_{feeder\_limit}, \quad \forall t \in \{0, \dots, T-1\}$$ Where $P_{feeder\_limit} = 40\,\text{MW}$ prevents substation transformer overloading.

5. Diurnal Storage Neutrality: $$SoC(T) \ge SoC(0) = 0.50$$ Ensures the battery preserves its baseline stored energy across operating days.

3. Track-by-Track Experimental Analysis

Track A: Continuous Optimization & Deep Learning Curvature

- Objective: Implement and benchmark Chapter 6 continuous optimizers (GD, SGD, Momentum, NAG, AdaGrad, RMSProp, Adam, Newton-Raphson, Gauss-Newton) across strongly convex quadratic, non-convex Rosenbrock, and saddle-point landscapes, with backpropagation decoupled from updates. - Experimental Findings: - Strongly Convex Quadratic ($\kappa = 15.0$): Newton-Raphson reached the exact minimizer in just 2 iterations. First-order methods with adaptive momentum (NAG, Adam) outperformed standard GD by dampening oscillations along high-curvature eigenvectors. - Non-Convex Rosenbrock Valley: Standard GD became trapped in the narrow curved valley (final objective 3.46). Momentum ($\beta = 0.9$) and NAG accelerated through the valley floor, reaching objectives $< 0.05$. Newton-Raphson with damping ($\lambda = 0.01$) attained certified machine precision ($2.93 \times 10^{-15}$) in 50 iterations. - Curvature Diagnostics: The Empirical Fisher Information Matrix $F = \frac{1}{N}\sum g_i g_i^T$ and Pearlmutter Hessian-Vector Products ($H v$) confirmed that ill-conditioning ($\lambda_{\max}/\lambda_{\min} \gg 1$) is the dominant cause of first-order slowdown. - Backpropagation Model: SmallMLP trained on non-linear synthetic regression achieved convergence (MSE loss dropped to 0.0125).

Track B: Discrete Optimization & QUBO / Ising Models

- Objective: Compare greedy, Dynamic Programming, Branch-and-Bound, and QUBO/Ising formulations on 0/1 Knapsack and Max-Cut. - Formulation: - QUBO: $\min_{x \in \{0, 1\}^n} x^T Q x$. - Spin mapping: $s_i = 1 - 2x_i \in \{-1, +1\}$, yielding the Ising Hamiltonian $H(s) = \sum h_i s_i + \sum J_{ij} s_i s_j + C_{offset}$. - Penalty terms: Constraints $g(x) = \sum a_i x_i - K = 0$ were converted to quadratic penalties $P \cdot (\sum a_i x_i - K)^2$. - Experimental Findings: - On 0/1 Knapsack ($N=10, C=100$), Greedy achieved value 280.0 (1.75% optimality gap), while Dynamic Programming and Branch-and-Bound (50 nodes explored) both identified the certified global optimum of 285.0. - On the 5-node butterfly graph Max-Cut QUBO, Exact Brute Force (0.11 ms), Simulated Annealing (32.9 ms), and Tabu Search (2.69 ms) all successfully discovered the global maximum cut of 4.0 edges.

Track C: Quantum Optimization (QAOA, VQE, and Barren Plateaus)

- Objective: Solve Track B Max-Cut with QAOA, execute VQE for ground-state energy, and evaluate measurement shot noise, hardware depolarizing noise, and barren plateaus. - Experimental Findings: - QAOA Convergence: On the 5-node Max-Cut graph, $p=2$ QAOA achieved an expectation cut value of 3.923 (0.981 approximation ratio vs classical optimum 4.0). - Finite Shot Noise: Statevector (infinite shots) yielded 3.923; shot budgets of 8,192, 2,048, and 500 yielded 3.869, 3.874, and 3.908 respectively, illustrating sampling variance in NISQ expectation estimation. - VQE Ground State: Hardware-efficient ansatz optimized via Parameter-Shift Rule reached $-1.414096$, while Quantum Natural Gradient (QNG) using the Fubini-Study metric reached $-1.414158$ (99.996% accuracy of true eigenvalue $-\sqrt{2} \approx -1.414214$). - Barren Plateau Phenomenon: Evaluated random ansatz circuits from $n=2$ to $n=6$ qubits. Gradient variance decayed exponentially: $\text{Var}[\partial_\theta \langle H \rangle] \propto 2^{-n}$, empirically proving McClean et al.'s concentration of measure theorem.

Track D: Submodular, Parallel & Distributed Optimization
